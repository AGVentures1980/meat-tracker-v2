BRASA FIELD OBSERVATION #013

THE CORNERSTONE: THE AGGREGATE RECEIVING TRAP

If you are signing protein delivery sheets by counting boxes instead of weighing them, you are leaving gross margin on the dock.

Catch-weight proteins—like tenderloins, ribeyes, and ribs—are billed to the decimal point. Yet, on a busy Friday morning delivery, the default operational behavior is speed: the clerk counts the boxes, checks them off the invoice, and signs the sheet.

• Counting cartons does not verify actual net weight.
• Distributors count on busy docks to skip physical scale checks.
• Unverified catch-weights lead to systematically padded invoices.
• Minor fractional weight losses compound into major EBITDA leaks.

Your receiving dock is not just a shipping drop-point. It is a cash register that only flows outward. If you do not verify the weight of every box at the moment it crosses the threshold, you are paying for meat you never received.

"WE PAY FOR THE PALLET. WE LOSE IN THE BOXES."

Protect margins where they enter the building. Weigh the box.
