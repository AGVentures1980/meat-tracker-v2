THE CORNERSTONE: THE OVER-PORTIONING ILLUSION

In high-volume restaurant operations, over-portioning is rarely a deliberate act of theft. More often, it is a compensation mechanism for process failure.

When service times drag, or food quality is inconsistent, kitchen lines often over-portion premium ingredients to appease guests. It is an expensive operational shortcut that attempts to buy guest satisfaction at the expense of gross margin.

• Over-portioning compensates for execution inconsistencies on the line.
• Plate overloading is used to mask service latency or product quality issues.
• Visual portioning by line staff degrades theoretical cost targets shift-by-shift.
• True consistency is what guests value; portion sizes must be predictable.

"UNMEASURED PORTIONING MASKS PROCESS DRIFT."

Portion governance stabilizes margins and protects guest experience.
