## 1. Value Hypotheses Ranking

To define BRASA's primary commercial identity, we rank the eight potential operational value drivers from highest potential economic impact (the largest margin leak) to lowest:

```
[Rank 1: Portion Control / Depletion Variance]
                     ▼
[Rank 2: Yield Variance / Processing Waste]
                     ▼
[Rank 3: Supplier Shortages / Receiving Drift]
                     ▼
[Rank 4: Waste Reduction / Spoilage]
                     ▼
[Rank 5: Production Overrun / Prep Errors]
                     ▼
[Rank 6: Inventory Accuracy / Stock Control]
                     ▼
[Rank 7: Count Compliance / Deadline Locks]
                     ▼
[Rank 8: Receiving Compliance / Scan Rates]
```

### 1. Portion Control / Depletion Variance (Rank 1)
*   **Description:** The difference between standard expected portion weight per customer and the actual portion weight sent to the floor.
*   **Leak Impact:** Highly critical. Small over-portioning errors (e.g. 0.1 lb per customer) compound across millions of guests annually, representing the largest single cash leakage source.

### 2. Yield Variance / Processing Waste (Rank 2)
*   **Description:** Raw meat lost at the butcher block during trimming due to sub-spec supplier cuts (excessive fat/gristle) or poor cutting discipline.
*   **Leak Impact:** High. Directly affects the cost of goods sold (COGS) before the protein ever reaches the kitchen.

### 3. Supplier Shortages / Receiving Drift (Rank 3)
*   **Description:** Mismatch between the billed invoice weight and actual scale weight verified at the receiving dock.
*   **Leak Impact:** Moderate. Highly auditable and recoverable via credit memos, but the total annual volume variance is typically smaller than internal yield waste.

### 4. Waste Reduction / Spoilage (Rank 4)
*   **Description:** Meat discarded due to poor rotation (FIFO failure) or temperature abuse.
*   **Leak Impact:** Moderate. Important for compliance, but typically managed well by standard kitchen SOPs.

Below is the estimated annual economic impact of each value vector calculated across a typical **50-store upscale rodizio network** (averaging $20,000,000 in annual protein spend):

### Economic Realities:
*   **Internal Yield vs. Inbound Receiving:** Internal prep yield waste ($400,000) and portion overage ($500,000) represent **4.5x more financial exposure** than simple supplier short-shipping credits ($200,000). 
*   **Conclusion:** While supplier shortages provide the fastest "credit-memo proof" of value, the core economic engine of BRASA lies inside the kitchen walls (butcher block and portion control).

## 4. Pilot Discovery Plan: Stamford

To determine which value vector is strongest in the field, the **Stamford (ID: 4)** pilot must execute a three-phase value discovery audit:

```
[Phase 1: Silent Baseline] ➔ [Phase 2: Segmented Analysis] ➔ [Phase 3: ROI Attribution]
```

### Phase 1 — Silent Baseline (Days 1–7)
Collect raw telemetry without active alerts. Establish the three baseline variances simultaneously:
1.  **Inbound Receiving Variance ($V_r$):** Billed vs dock weight.
2.  **Processing Yield Variance ($V_y$):** Actual butcher trim vs standard target.
3.  **Depletion Variance ($V_d$):** POS sales depletion vs actual kitchen usage.

### Phase 2 — Segmented Analysis (Days 8–21)
Enable active alerts for each vector sequentially and track the slope of compliance recovery. Compare how quickly GMs react to weight warnings vs. butcher trim alerts.

### Phase 3 — ROI Attribution (Days 22–30)
Run a regression analysis on the compiled pilot data. Calculate which value vector generated the highest absolute dollar savings for Stamford.

## 5. Positioning Risk Analysis

```
Narrow Positioning (Supplier Shortage)  ➔ Low TAM, Vendor pushback, Churn Risk
Broad Positioning (Operational Yield)   ➔ High TAM, BOH Standard, Continuous Value
```

### The Risk of a "Supplier Shortage" Positioning:
*   **Low Ceiling on TAM:** If BRASA is just a "supplier short-weight auditor," the customer will see us as a temporary tool. Once suppliers realize they are being monitored and adjust their packing scales, weight shortages will decrease, and the client will cancel BRASA.
*   **Supplier Conflict:** Suppliers may push back and threaten to terminate contracts, forcing the restaurant to side with their distributor.

### The Opportunity of a "Margin Recovery & Yield" Positioning:
*   **Continuous Value:** Internal kitchen waste, butcher trim yields, and portion overage are continuous operational challenges that never disappear. Positioning BRASA as an **Operational Yield Intelligence Platform** guarantees we remain in place permanently as a core BOH operating system.

## 6. Recommended Primary Value Proposition

Based on the economic modeling and positioning risks, BRASA must not be marketed as a supplier audit tool.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Authoritative Recommendation:**  
> BRASA should ultimately be positioned and known as an **Operational Yield Intelligence Platform**.

### Core Rationale:
1.  **Maximum TAM:** Positioned at the intersection of receiving dock verification, butcher prep yield, and portion control.
2.  **Continuous ROI:** Internal operational slippage (butcher trim and plate portioning) is a permanent operational challenge that requires continuous, automated software monitoring.
3.  **Unified BOH OS:** Ties the receiving dock directly to the kitchen block and the POS register, making the software indispensable for executive operators.
