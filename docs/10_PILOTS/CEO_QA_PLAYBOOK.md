## 1. Financial Objections

### Q1: What is the direct return on investment (ROI)?
*   **What they are really asking:** Are we going to lose money on this software?
*   **Ideal Answer:** *"Based on pilot baseline results, a 50-store upscale brand recovers an average of $200,000 annually in supplier shortages, and up to $900,000 in internal kitchen waste. At $650/month per store, the ROI exceeds 5x in direct margin recovery."*
*   **Bad Answer:** *"It's hard to calculate ROI because it depends on how much your staff uses the scanner."*
*   **Follow-up Question:** *"What is your current annual spend on premium beef ribs and picanha, and what does a 1% recovery on that translate to for your EBITDA?"*

### Q2: Is this a capital expense (CapEx) or operating expense (OpEx)?
*   **What they are really asking:** How does this affect our balance sheet?
*   **Ideal Answer:** *"It is structured as a standard operating expense (OpEx) monthly software subscription. The initial pilot uses a small, one-time setup fee ($1,550 per store) to cover terminal delivery and calibration, requiring no CapEx allocation."*
*   **Bad Answer:** *"It depends on how we classify the mobile terminals you need to buy."*
*   **Follow-up Question:** *"Do you prefer to review this as a regional store-level operational expense or as a corporate procurement cost?"*

### Q3: We have a flat corporate spending freeze. Why should we fund this?
*   **What they are really asking:** Is this critical enough to bypass our current budget freeze?
*   **Ideal Answer:** *"Because we do not increase your costs; we recover existing cash leakage. If you have a spending freeze, protecting raw margins is the fastest way to support your EBITDA. We identify supplier short-ships and recover credit memos immediately."*
*   **Bad Answer:** *"You should bypass the freeze because our software is cheap and easy to try."*
*   **Follow-up Question:** *"If we find zero cash leakage during our 30-day Stamford pilot, we exit without billing you further. If we recover cash, the savings fund the subscription. Shall we verify?"*

### Q4: How do supplier credit memos actually get processed?
*   **What they are really asking:** Is this going to require manual paperwork for our accounts payable team?
*   **Ideal Answer:** *"No. The system generates a standardized PDF weight shortage receipt at the dock. The GM sends this to the distributor rep. The rep issues a credit memo matching the receipt, which automatically reconciles with your accounts payable ledger."*
*   **Bad Answer:** *"Our software automatically emails the supplier and demands they credit your bank account."*
*   **Follow-up Question:** *"How does your accounting team reconcile invoice weights with actual received weights today?"*

### Q5: If food costs drop, does that automatically translate to cash flow?
*   **What they are really asking:** Is this paper savings or real money?
*   **Ideal Answer:** *"It is direct cash flow. If a supplier bills for 50 lbs and delivers 48.5 lbs, and we recover that credit, you do not pay for that 1.5 lbs of missing beef. That is real cash remaining in your operating account."*
*   **Bad Answer:** *"Yes, because lower food cost percentages make your store look more profitable on paper."*
*   **Follow-up Question:** *"How many credit claims did your Stamford store file last month for weight shortages?"*

### Q6: What is the cost of subscription after the pilot?
*   **What they are really asking:** Are you going to hit us with a massive fee once we are dependent on the software?
*   **Ideal Answer:** *"Our post-pilot subscription is $650 per month per store. There are no hidden fees. We align our contract conversion thresholds to prove a minimum 3x return on that subscription before rollout."*
*   **Bad Answer:** *"It depends on how many boxes you scan and how many users you add to the portal."*
*   **Follow-up Question:** *"Does a flat monthly fee match your regional budget structures, or do you prefer a value-based model?"*
